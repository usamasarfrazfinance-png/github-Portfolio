EQS Group

Overview

EQS was a long-running engagement covering the full Dynamics 365 CE stack: plugin development, opportunity automation, a customer-facing portal, third-party integrations, and reporting. The scope grew over time from individual customisations into a set of interlocking systems that ran the sales and support side of the business.

Power Pages customer portal (HelpDesk)

I built a self-service support portal from scratch, giving customers local account registration and sign-in, a live request dashboard showing case status, and routed intake for different request types (bug reports, on-call service, webcast and conference requests, general questions). It replaced email-based support with a trackable case pipeline synced back to Dynamics 365.

Opportunity automation and won-opportunity notifications

I built out several linked Power Automate flows around the Opportunity lifecycle. An orchestration flow evaluated newly created opportunities against fee and ARR conditions and routed each one to the right downstream flow. A separate flow, triggered from CRM on close-won, handled business-unit-based recipient logic, fee and licence calculations, and pushed Microsoft Teams notifications, with specific rules for freemium, sustainability, and data-privacy scenarios.

On top of that, I worked on notification logic that split won opportunities into standard and partner categories, then further into region groups (global excluding DACH, DACH, partner excluding DACH, partner DACH), using contract-partner conditions and de-duplicating by parent account so the resulting counts and notifications were accurate per group.

Contract co-terming

Co-terming was one of the larger pieces of work: consolidating multiple related contracts tied to a master opportunity into a single new opportunity when renewal dates aligned. The process identified qualifying master and related opportunities (won, not terminated, auto-renewal on, matching currency), built a new consolidated opportunity carrying the combined contract data, copied and merged opportunity products (combining lines with matching product IDs and excluding terminated ones), then terminated the originals and linked their contracts across to the new record.

Alongside the build, I worked through the business rules with stakeholders: which opportunities counted as subcontracts, how end-of-contract dates should be calculated, what statuses the copied products should carry, and which source record should win when several opportunities were being merged.

Dow Jones risk and compliance screening

I designed and built a compliance-screening integration against the Dow Jones Risk & Compliance API, run through five linked Power Automate flows. Two authentication flows handled the account and opportunity triggers, exchanging credentials for a bearer token and passing accounts to two reusable child flows that screened against Adverse Media and Sanctions lists.

The flow created a Dow Jones "case," attached a person or entity association, linked the two, retrieved matched profile IDs, pulled the full profile detail, and wrote the results back onto the CRM Account. A third flow supported manual re-screening of existing accounts from a view or a single-record ribbon button, for cases outside the automatic create/update triggers.

Why it mattered: this gave the compliance team a way to screen new and existing accounts against sanctions and adverse-media data without leaving CRM, instead of running manual lookups outside the system.

Remittance reporting and SharePoint migration

I built and maintained an SSRS remittance report, including a plugin that generated the report as PDF for one or multiple records, returned it as a byte array through a custom action, and fed that into Power Automate to save the file to SharePoint and make it downloadable from Power Pages.

Separately, I worked on migrating CRM file attachments off the Annotation entity and onto SharePoint, over 25,000 records, which meant building proper pagination and high-volume retrieval instead of pulling everything in one pass, while keeping the relationships between the original CRM records and their migrated files intact.

Broader scope

Beyond these, the engagement covered a long list of smaller but real components: a structured plugin architecture (base classes, pre/post images, tracing, early-bound entities) used consistently across the codebase; Business Process Flow and form-load JavaScript refactored into shared reusable functions instead of duplicated logic; Power Pages work including a table-based class schedule view with pagination fixes for a screen that was loading thousands of records at once, login tracking, and Liquid-based portal customisation; lead and partner qualification logic; opportunity cloning; quote and document generation; ClickDimensions and Mailchimp-driven lead journeys; case-queue routing; and Power BI reporting embedded directly in Dynamics for customer service metrics like first-touch resolution.
